    Graduating from Kookmin University: Building a Research Path in Vision AI and GeoAI

    September 24, 2026 EARTH OBSERVATION, GEOAI, GKS, GRADUATION, RESEARCH
    3 min.

    In June 2026, I completed my M.S. in Data Science / Big Data at Kookmin University. This closes an important chapter that began when I came to South Korea through the Global Korea Scholarship (GKS) and gradually developed a research direction connecting computer vision, Earth observation, remote sensing, and GeoAI.

    The degree gave me more than a qualification. It taught me how to turn a broad idea into a research question, audit difficult datasets, design reproducible experiments, interpret results carefully, and communicate a contribution through code and scientific writing.

    Finding My Research Direction

    My graduate research centred on change detection from bi-temporal remote-sensing imagery. I worked with Siamese CNNs, attention mechanisms, transformer-based baselines, semantic segmentation, ablation studies, threshold selection, and multi-benchmark evaluation.

    That work led to CoSA: Correlation-Guided Change Attention with Learnable Residual Gating for Remote Sensing Change Detection, published in IEEE Access, and UrbanCDNet, a preprint focused on appearance-robust and boundary-aware urban building change detection.

    The same research mindset also shaped a computer-vision project on data-efficient crosswalk segmentation from overhead CCTV. Using 241 manually annotated CCTV images and 5,926 unlabelled frames, the work achieved 88.91% IoU on held-out, manually labelled validation data while maintaining real-time inference.

    Beyond these core projects, collaboration expanded my perspective. Work on African rainfall-anomaly retrieval connected representation learning with environmental data and strengthened my interest in climate and environmental AI for African contexts.

    Research Beyond Benchmarks

    During the degree, I became increasingly interested in the space between a model and the real decision it is supposed to support. Projects such as flood-impact mapping in Beledweyne, urban morphology analysis in Seoul, and live urban-data dashboards helped me think about geospatial workflows as complete systems: data acquisition, cleaning, modelling, spatial analysis, validation, visualisation, and communication.

    This is the direction I want to keep developing. I am especially interested in research where satellite, aerial, street-level, and environmental data can be combined to understand how cities and landscapes change over time.

    What Comes Next

    I am now based in Somalia and preparing for the next stage: PhD research in computer vision, Earth observation, GeoAI, multi-temporal analysis, and urban or environmental AI.

    The questions that motivate me are broad but connected:

    • How can change-detection systems remain reliable across seasons, sensors, and geographic regions?
    • How can limited annotations be used more effectively through domain adaptation, pseudo-labeling, and self-supervised learning?
    • How can uncertainty and failure cases be communicated clearly in geospatial decision-support systems?
    • How can advanced spatial AI methods be made useful for African environmental and urban contexts?

    Graduation is a milestone, but it does not feel like the end of the work. It feels like the point where the research direction became clear.

    I am grateful to the people who supported this journey—my collaborators, teachers, colleagues, friends, and family—and to the GKS programme and Kookmin University for making this chapter possible.

    The next goal is to keep building careful, reproducible research that connects strong computer-vision methods with real spatial and environmental problems.
